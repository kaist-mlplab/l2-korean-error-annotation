Refining Word-Based Grammatical Error Annotation for L2 Korean

Reconstructed NIKL learner-corpus targets, a Korean ERRANT-style edit scheme, and a multi-reference KoLLA set for Korean grammatical error correction

arXiv 2026

Jungyeul Park1,†,*, Kyungtae Lim1,†, Wonjun Oh1,†, Benjamin Nguyen2, Zihao Huang2, Mengyang Qiu3, Jayoung Song4,*

1Korea Advanced Institute of Science & Technology (KAIST), South Korea 2The University of British Columbia, Canada 3Saint Elizabeth University, USA 4The Pennsylvania State University, USA

†Equal contribution   *Corresponding authors

Paper (arXiv) PDF

Abstract

Korean grammatical error correction (K-GEC) presents a structural mismatch between word-based evaluation and the morpheme-level locus of many learner errors. Postpositions and verbal endings are bound to lexical hosts, but they encode grammatical relations that must be represented in correction and evaluation. This paper refines word-based grammatical error annotation for L2 Korean by addressing three connected problems in existing resources: surface target realization, Korean-specific edit annotation, and single-reference evaluation. We reconstruct target sentences from the National Institute of Korean Language (NIKL) L2 corpus under morphologically constrained realization rules and convert its morpheme-level annotations into word-level m2 edits. We then define a Korean ERRANT-style annotation scheme that preserves the MRU core while distinguishing functional morpheme errors, spelling errors, word boundary errors, and word order errors. We also augment the KoLLA corpus with an additional reference correction, yielding a multi-reference evaluation setting for Korean GEC. Empirical validation shows that the refined NIKL targets yield lower perplexity, the converted m2 files achieve higher agreement with source-target edit representations, and the refined resources improve KoBART-based correction under the same model setting. Multi-reference KoLLA evaluation further reduces the penalty imposed on valid corrections that diverge from a single reference, especially for neural and prompted GEC systems. These results show that Korean GEC evaluation depends not only on correction models, but also on reference data and edit annotations that reflect Korean morphology, spacing, and correction variability.

Refining the Resources

Many Korean learner errors sit inside the eojeol, in postpositions and verbal endings bound to a lexical host, while GEC is trained and scored on surface words. The paper addresses three connected problems:

  1. Target reconstruction (NIKL). The NIKL L2 corpus gives corrections only as morpheme sequences, so target sentences must be rebuilt. Contraction and irregular-conjugation rules are applied only to predicates and only before endings that license the alternation (avoiding errors such as 아빠에게 → *아빠게); the few lemmas with ambiguous conjugation classes (e.g. 묻다 ‘bury’ vs. ‘ask’, 22 occurrences) are checked by hand.
  2. Korean ERRANT-style edits. Morpheme-level annotations are converted into word-level m2 edits that keep ERRANT's Missing / Replacement / Unnecessary core but expose bound morphology, e.g. R:NOUN -> NOUN+ADP for a missing particle, with ADP for postpositions and PART for verbal endings. Further subtypes mark spelling (R:SPELL), word order (R:WO), and word boundary (R:WB) errors, assigned by a deterministic classifier.
  3. Multi-reference KoLLA. The 100-essay KoLLA learner corpus receives a second, independently produced reference correction that follows a minimal sentence-level correction principle, alongside the original, more context-enriched layer.

Main Results

  • More accurate and more fluent targets. Surface reconstruction accuracy on NIKL rises from 95.21% to 98.77% for all eojeols and from 91.34% to 97.85% for verbs and adjectives; on KLUE, accuracy on contracted or variant eojeols rises from 61.07% to 94.16%. Refined targets have lower perplexity under all four language models (e.g., kanana2-30b-a3b 163.28 → 153.99).
  • Edits that match the corrections. Agreement between converted m2 edits and edits extracted from source–target pairs improves from F0.5 0.8523 to 0.9304 (precision 0.9332, recall 0.9198).
  • Better supervision. With the same model and training setup, KoBART trained on refined NIKL targets (KoBART+) improves F0.5 from 0.4548 to 0.4900 and GLEU from 49.82 to 50.51.
  • Multiple references matter. On KoLLA, KoBART+ scores F0.5 0.2700 against the original reference, 0.2980 against the new one, and 0.3778 when either may match. Prompted LLMs also score higher on multi-reference KoLLA than on single-reference NIKL (best: kanana2-30b-a3b, 0.5901 vs. 0.4643).
